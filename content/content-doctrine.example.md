# Content doctrine — attentioneering

Derived from **`$1M Attention.docx`** (Starter Story). This file is the north star every
skill in this repo already claims to read. It did not exist until now, so every skill's
STEP 0 was silently failing.

Example screenshots from the doc: `reference/attention-examples/`

---

## The thesis

> **Attentioneering (n.):** the strategic use of algorithms, psychological tactics, and
> content optimization to capture and sustain attention for a brand, business, or product.

> "A shitty product with attention will always beat a good product without attention."

Attention is not a launch event. Get attention **before** the product exists, **as** you
build it, **during** launch, and **after**. Always be getting attention.

---

## The four rules that override everything

### 1. Intent beats engagement

> "Engagement is worth nothing unless it has intent."

Every post must drive intent toward the product. Judge a post by whether it moves someone
toward the thing you sell — **not** by views, likes, or follower count. A post about your
breakfast will not sell B2B SaaS.

**Applied:** before writing, answer *what do I want the reader to do?* If the answer is
"nothing", do not write the post.

### 2. LEHI — lowest effort, highest impact

> "How can you get the most eyeballs on your thing, with the least amount of work?"

Pick formats that fit the operator's actual skill. Good writer → LinkedIn. Good on video
→ short form. Meme instinct → X. Big network → direct outreach, not broadcast.

### 3. Write in first person, as the builder

Every winning example in the doc is the founder speaking as themselves:

- "**I made** a privacy-first…"
- "Spent the last 6 months building X, **what do you think?**"
- "In X days, **I built** this thing"
- "**I'm 17 y/o** and I built this"
- "**I just quit my job**"

The power comes from a real person having done a real thing. Vulnerability, effort and
stakes are the mechanism — an observer describing a trend has none of them.

### 4. Do not let AI write the final copy

The doc is blunt about this:

> "**Rule of thumb: Do not use AI.** People will see right through this, it comes off
> ingenuine, and they will not engage with your stuff. Yes, you can and should use AI to
> find ideas, improve a certain word, or whatever. But I urge you to spend your deep work
> writing great copy."

**How this repo should therefore work:**

| AI does | The human does |
|---|---|
| Find the topic, thread, or data | Supply the true story, numbers, feelings |
| Pick the format from the library below | Write or rewrite the words |
| Draft a structural skeleton | Final pass so it sounds like you |
| Build the visual asset | Approve before it ships |
| Schedule it | — |

A generated post that claims a first-person experience the founder did not have is not a
style problem, it is a lie in public under their name. **Anything first-person and
factual — revenue, timelines, "I quit my job" — must come from the human.**

## Format library

17 formats from the doc. Each is a *template*: keep the mechanic, swap in your product.
Numbering follows the doc (it has two #9s and #12–#16 are unfinished stubs there).

### #1 — Personal network: "It's done."
**Channels:** LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X
**Why it works:** you are telling your network you worked hard on something, and being
brave enough to post it knowing it might fail. People reward that with engagement, which
feeds the algorithm.
**How:** emphasise how hard it was. Bring the drama. Attach a photo of **you**. Go light
on emoji.

### #2 — Reddit: "…what do you think?"
**Why it works:** the title carries it. Same product, two titles:
- ✗ "Hacksource — programming tutorial search engine" → 0 upvotes
- ✓ "Spent the last 6 months building a programming tutorial search engine, what do you think?"

Personal, asks for feedback, human.
**How:** find subreddits where your customer actually is. Expect self-promo friction; try
several.

### #3 — Hacker News: "I made…"
**Why it works:** "I made" signals a real person. Then pander to the audience's values —
"privacy-first" lands on HN because they love privacy and distrust big tech.
**How:** find the one feature that maps to that crowd's values and lead with it.

### #4 — X: ask the question your product answers
**Why it works:** the question names the pain directly, then the product is the answer.
One example became a $1M/year business.
**How:** ask the question at the root of the pain. Link the solution. Add a visual.

### #5 — LinkedIn: "In X days, I built this thing"
**Why it works:** effort + a scannable bullet format that tells people exactly what they
get. Close on why it matters to everyone.
**How:** state what you built and how long it took. Add a product preview image.

### #6 — X: "I just quit my job" / life update
**Why it works:** a real stake in the story. Followers rally around it.
**How:** you need not quit anything — any genuine life update in this shape works.

### #7 — DMs / email: brute force your personal network
**Why it works:** Codie Sanchez got her first 1,000 subscribers manually emailing old
contacts. Modern-day door knocking.
**How:** work through LinkedIn connections, your phone contacts, recent texts, email, IG
followers. Personal message, ask them to share.

### #8 — Cold email: "I'm offering this for free"
**Why it works:** free removes risk, and being honest about your motive ("I get to build
my portfolio") makes people *want* to help.
**How:** research 5–10 companies you'd want in your portfolio. Less is more. You don't
even need a finished product.

### #9 — Quora / Reddit: answer a direct-intent question
**Why it works:** these rank in search and keep paying out for years. One real business
runs $24K MRR off this.
**How:** `site:quora.com [question your product solves]`, answer the top-ranked ones.
Only worth it where the question carries **buying intent**.

### #10 — Indie Hackers: "My first $X in revenue!"
**Why it works:** not a one-shot post — a series of vulnerable updates that builds a
small community around you.
**How:** engage with others first. Support people. Study founders one stage ahead of you.

### #9b — LinkedIn: humbling story + lessons, with a photo
**Why it works:** people love a humbling story, and the photo carries it — the air
mattress, the grind.
**How:** humble beginnings → how you got through it → where you are now (link the
product) → a few digestible lessons. Reply to every comment.

### #11 — TikTok / Reels / Shorts: simple product demo
**Why it works:** one founder's minute-long video drove tens of thousands of signups.
**How:** show the product doing the thing. Nothing fancier.

### #17 — X: "I'm 17 y/o and I built this"
**Why it works:** underdog story plus proof. The Loom video shows a real builder rather
than someone describing an idea. Short, honest, personal.
**How:** lean into whatever makes you the underdog — young, inexperienced, starting out.
Record a short Loom of the product.

### Stubs (unfinished in the source doc)
#12 turn bad news into good news · #13 hot take that drives intent · #14 POV ·
#15 LinkedIn slideshow "how I built it" · #16 start a conversation.
Flagged as future additions in the doc, along with: build-in-public posts per platform,
Product Hunt launch with demo video, revenue/Stripe screenshot updates, running ads.

## Choosing a format

1. **What is the intent?** What should the reader do after reading?
2. **What is true right now?** A shipped thing, a number, a failure, a milestone. No
   invented stories.
3. **Which format fits that truth?** Milestone → #1 or #5. Feedback wanted → #2. Dev
   audience → #3. Pain-first → #4. Underdog → #17. Visual product → #11.
4. **LEHI check:** is this the cheapest way to reach these eyeballs?

## Content mix — do not only sell

Constant product promotion burns an audience. But the doc's Rule 1 still applies: every
post needs **intent**. The resolution is that most posts earn the right to the few that
sell, and the non-selling ones still point somewhere.

### Pillars, per 10 posts

| # | Pillar | What it is | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **Product** | Relo directly. Formats #1 #4 #5 #11 #17 | Signup |
| 3 | **AI news with implications** | What just shipped and what it means for the reader's work, income, skills. Never a dry relay | Authority, follow |
| 2 | **Lessons from building** | Something you got wrong, a number you learned, a decision you reversed. Build in public | Trust, follow |
| 1 | **Data visual** | One striking stat about AI, work, or video. Infographic | Saves, shares |
| 1 | **Engagement** | A poll or a genuine question with no obvious answer | Reach, comments |

Product is 30 percent, not 100. The other 70 percent is why anyone is still listening
when you do post about Relo.

### The rule that keeps non-product posts honest

A non-product post still needs a destination. It does not need a pitch. Acceptable:

- the profile bio carries the link
- one closing line that fits naturally
- nothing at all, when the post is purely audience building

Not acceptable: a post about your breakfast. The doc is explicit that engagement without
intent is worthless.

### Rotation and repetition

`content-history.json` is the memory. Before writing anything, read it and honour:

- **topic** — no repeat within 21 days
- **doc_format** — no repeat within 7 days
- **hook_style** — last run's style is banned; any style used 3+ times in the last 7
  entries is banned
- **pillar** — no more than 2 of the same pillar in any 5 consecutive posts

Append every post you create. Never delete rows: the history *is* the dedup mechanism.

## Accounts and voice — which identity posts where

This is not a style preference. Posting company content in a personal voice, or personal
content from a brand handle, reads as a mistake to the audience.

| Platform | Account | Identity | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | **Personal** (Vraj Patel) | a founder talking to peers | **First person, "I"**. Formats #1 #5 #9b #17. Personal stakes, opinions, what you got wrong. A photo of you beats a graphic. |
| **X** | **Company** (@relo_video) | the product's own account | **Brand voice**. "we" for the company, or no pronoun at all. Sharp, short, industry-aware. Formats #4 #11 #13. Personal-life stories do not belong here. |
| **Instagram** | **Company** (@relo_video) | the product's own account | **Brand voice**, visual first. Carousels and video, not text. Educational or industry, not diary. |

### Consequences

- A "the night I almost quit" story is **LinkedIn only**. It is nonsense from a brand handle.
- A hot take on where AI video is going works on **X** in brand voice, and on **LinkedIn**
  in first person. Same idea, two different writes. Never the same text on both.
- Instagram gets the least text and the most design. A text-only Instagram post is a
  wasted slot.

## Format variety — vary the container, not just the words

Rotate the asset type as deliberately as the pillar. Check `content-history.json` before
choosing.

| Asset | Use for | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Text only** | opinions, questions, quick takes | at most **1 in 3** posts |
| **Single image** | one stat, one comparison, one strong idea | good default for LinkedIn |
| **Carousel** | a sequence, a breakdown, a mapped shift | strongest on LinkedIn and Instagram |
| **Video** | product in motion, demos | strongest on Instagram and X |

**No more than two consecutive posts with the same asset type on the same platform.**
Build assets with `build_assets.cjs` and a brief. A post that would be stronger as a
visual should not ship as text because text was faster.

### Visual style

**Light backgrounds by default.** Warm paper `#F7F5F2`, ink `#0a0a0b`, gradient accents.
Set `"theme": "light"` in the brief's brand block; `build_assets.cjs` derives every text,
rule and chip colour from it. Reserve dark for when a specific piece calls for it.

### Subject matter — the audience is builders, not just video buyers

The reader is someone shipping a product with AI, usually alone or nearly. Video is one
of their problems, not their identity. Anything in the AI industry that changes how they
work is fair game, and the non-video topics usually travel further:

- **Building with AI** — agentic engineering, what the agent got wrong, reviewing code you
  did not write, technical debt from generated code
- **Shipping solo** — distribution without a team, doing your own design, marketing, video
- **AI industry shifts** — a model being deprecated, pricing changes, what a launch means
  for small builders rather than for enterprises
- **The craft** — what "good" means when anyone can generate the first draft
- **Video and creator tooling** — the category Relo sits in

Rule of thumb: **at most one in three posts should be about video at all.** Own the
audience's whole problem, not just the slice your product bills for. A post that helps
someone ship better earns the right to the post that sells them something.

**Accuracy is non-negotiable.** Prefer a primary source (the company's own announcement,
the person's own post) over a stats-roundup blog. When a figure only exists in secondary
reporting, say "reported" in the copy and on the asset. Never restate a modelled estimate
as measured data.

## Posting times — target US Eastern, enter IST

The audience is US/North America. The machine and the browser sessions are in IST, and
**every scheduler uses the browser's local timezone** (LinkedIn says so out loud: "India
Standard Time, based on your location"). So `posts.json` must hold the **IST wall-clock
time** that corresponds to the Eastern target.

### Peak windows (US Eastern)

| Platform | Best slots | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Wed 16:00, Fri 15:00, Fri 16:00 | Sprout Social, ~2B engagements across 307k profiles, Nov 2025 - Feb 2026 |
| **X** | Thu 09:00 (highest median engagement) | Sprout Social |
| **Instagram** | Thu 09:00, Wed 12:00, Wed 18:00 | Buffer, 9.6M posts |
| General fallback | Tue-Thu, 09:00-12:00 | Sprout Social |

LinkedIn moved later than the workday in 2026. Its peaks are now mid-to-late afternoon,
not 9am.

### The conversion

**IST = Eastern + 9:30 during EDT, + 10:30 during EST.** US clocks change on 1 Nov 2026,
so a hardcoded offset is right for half the year and wrong for the other half.

Never do this by hand. Run:

```bash
python research/retime.py     # edit the PLAN block, it converts and writes posts.json
```

It uses real tz data, warns on any slot that lands in the past or off a 15-minute
boundary, and records the Eastern target alongside the IST time in each entry as
`target_et` so the intent stays visible.

### Consequence worth knowing

A 16:00 ET LinkedIn slot is **01:30 IST the next morning**. That is fine, because the
post is scheduled rather than published by hand, but the Chrome session must still be
running when `/schedule-posts` is executed, not when the post goes live.

## Cadence — how much, per platform

Volume is not equal across platforms. This is the target, per week:

| Platform | Cadence | Per week | Account |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | 3-4 per week | 3-4 | personal |
| **X** | 2-3 per day | ~15-21 | company |
| **Instagram** | 1-2 per day | ~7-14 | company |

**X carries the volume.** That is deliberate: it is the cheapest place to post often, the
format is short, and the feed rewards frequency. LinkedIn is the opposite - fewer, longer,
higher effort, and over-posting there costs reach.

### What this means for a batch

A single `/create-posts` run should produce roughly a week of X, a week of Instagram, and
3-4 LinkedIn posts. In practice that is **X-heavy: build the X batch first**, then
Instagram, then LinkedIn last.

Do not manufacture nine near-identical X posts to hit a number. If there is not enough
genuine material for the full week, produce fewer and say so.

### Daily slots (US Eastern)

At 2-3 a day you need more than the single peak hour:

| Platform | Slots (ET) |
|---|---|
| **X** | 09:00 (peak), 13:00, 17:00 |
| **Instagram** | 09:00 (peak), 18:00 |
| **LinkedIn** | Wed 16:00, Fri 15:00, Tue 16:00 |

In IST that is X at 18:30, 22:30, and 02:30 next day; Instagram at 18:30 and 03:30 next
day. `research/retime.py` does the conversion.

### Dedup at high volume

The standard rules were written for three posts a run and do not survive fifteen. At
volume, apply them **per platform stream**, since a follower on X never sees the LinkedIn
queue:

| Field | Low volume (LinkedIn, Instagram) | High volume (X) |
|---|---|---|
| `topic` | no repeat within 21 days | no repeat within **7 days** |
| `doc_format` | no repeat within 7 days | no repeat within **2 days** |
| `hook_style` | last run banned, 3+ in last 7 banned | **no repeat within the same day** |
| `pillar` | max 2 of the same in any 5 | max 2 of the same **per day** |

A topic may legitimately appear once on each platform in the same week, written natively
for each. It may not appear twice in the same platform's stream in that week.

## Conflicts with the existing rules — read before editing skills

Two things in this doctrine directly contradict `commands/linkedin-content.md`, which
governs the current generators. **The conflicts are flagged, not silently resolved.**

### Conflict 1 — voice

| `commands/linkedin-content.md` | This doctrine |
|---|---|
| "Write in **third-person observer** voice. **No 'I' statements anywhere.**" | Every winning example is **first person**: "I made", "I built", "I quit" |

Both cannot hold. They serve different jobs:

- **Third person** suits a *media brand* commenting on an industry (@yourhandle's
  original AI-news positioning).
- **First person** suits a *founder promoting their own product* (Relo).

**Recommendation:** first person for anything promoting Relo; keep third person only if
@yourhandle continues as a separate observer/media account. Needs your call.

### Conflict 2 — AI-written copy

The doctrine says do not let AI write the final copy. This repo is an AI content
pipeline. The honest reconciliation is the split in Rule 4: **AI researches, structures,
builds assets and schedules; the human owns the words and the truth claims.**

That is a real constraint on how far this can be automated, and it is the doc's own
advice, not a limitation of the tooling.

## Still inherited from `commands/linkedin-content.md`

These are not in the source doc but remain good hygiene:

- No em-dashes
- Banned vocabulary: delve, leverage, game-changer, supercharge, tapestry, landscape
  (abstract), it's important to note, moreover, furthermore, …
- Banned patterns: "No X. No Y. Just Z.", "It's not just X, it's Y", "And here's the
  kicker", "Enter: [framework]", "The best part?"
- Specific numbers over adjectives
- Sentence case in headings
- One idea per paragraph
